# Make your first game

You can make a small game today. It does not have to be big. It does not have to be pretty. It has to be **yours**.

This page is for anyone who has never made a game before. You do not need to know how to code, and you do not need
an AI.

## Before you start

A grown-up starts the VEFR Studio on the computer once ([Getting started](../../GETTING_STARTED.md) shows how).
After that, everything happens in a web browser. The Studio is a workshop run by friendly folk from old Norse
stories. Each room helps with one part of your game.

## You are the boss of your game

Real game studios have a few jobs, and you can do all of them:

- **The boss** decides what the game is about. That's you. Helpers can suggest; you choose.
- **The idea friend** asks good questions so your idea gets clearer. That can be a friend, a grown-up, or one of
  the Studio's folk.
- **The builder** makes what the boss decided, one small piece at a time.
- **The players** try it and say what felt fun and what felt wrong.

## Six steps, round and round

Go round as many times as you like. Each time round, your game gets a little better.

1. **Dream.** Say your game in one sentence. *"A cat who runs a bakery has lost her rolling pin."*
2. **Shrink it.** Make it as small as it can be and still be your idea:
   - one place;
   - one person to meet;
   - one thing to find;
   - one reason to care.
3. **Make a little bit.** Use the Studio's rooms. Each room ends with something you can see.

   | Room | You make |
   |---|---|
   | **The Desk** | your game's name and your one sentence |
   | **The Map Room** | your place (the Studio checks you can walk everywhere) |
   | **The Folks** | one person, and the way they talk |
   | **The Library** | a note someone can find and read |
   | **The Vault** | one thing to find |

4. **Play it.** Walk around your game. Is it fun? Is anything confusing?
5. **Change one thing.** Just one, so you know what made the difference. Then play again.
6. **Share it.** At the Desk, press **Make shareable file**. You get one file that opens in any web browser, to send
   to a friend.

## Tips from people who make games

- **Start tiny.** Big games start as tiny tests.
- **A picture first is fine.** Draw it or make a quick picture before you build. A picture is a *mockup*, not a
  promise. A version you can try is a *prototype*. The one you choose to keep is *approved*.
- **Play it a lot.** Say out loud what you notice. That's called *playtesting*.
- **Write down what you learned**, so next time is easier.
- **If something breaks, it's not your fault.** It's a *bug*. Tell a grown-up, and they can report it.

## Words game makers use

| Word | What it means |
|---|---|
| **map** | where your game happens |
| **tile** | one square of ground on the map |
| **sprite** | a picture of a character or a thing |
| **NPC** | a person in the game who isn't you ("non-player character") |
| **mockup** | a picture of an idea: to look at, not to play |
| **prototype** | a rough version you can try |
| **playtest** | playing your game to find what to fix |
| **bug** | something that doesn't work the way it should |
| **ship** | share the finished game |

More of VEFR's words are in the [glossary](glossary.md). When you're ready for more rooms and more ideas, the
[Journey](journey.md) is the whole map.

## Grown-ups helping?

This page follows the same loop real game teams use: dream, shrink, make, play, change, share. You can help
most by asking good questions in steps 1 and 2, and by playing along in step 4. Let the young maker decide.
